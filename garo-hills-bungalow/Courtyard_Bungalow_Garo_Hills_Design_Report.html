1. The idea

The earlier design cost ₹ 4.69 crore. To bring everything on the plot under ₹ 50 lakh, the house is now one compact rectangle, 46.00 ft by 28.00 ft on its wall centre lines, under one simple hipped roof. Plain shapes are the cheapest to build, and also the strongest in an earthquake.

The plan has three bays. The middle bay is one open sitting-and-dining room, 15.62 ft wide and 27.25 ft deep, running from the front door on the north to the veranda on the south. The west bay holds the library-study at the front and your bedroom suite at the back. The east bay holds the shared toilet and bathroom in the north-east corner, the kitchen behind its own wall, and the guest bedroom with its bath. The two bedrooms are at opposite ends, both facing south for winter sun.

The rest of the hectare stays almost free to build: native grove left to grow, an orchard and kitchen garden planted from saplings, a gravel drive, a carport instead of a garage, and a chain-link fence instead of a masonry compound wall, with a short stone wall only at the gate.

At 14.00 cu ft a day the tank lasts 353.43 ÷ 14.00 = 25.24 days, so the spring or well line carries the dry months and the tank is the backup. Overflow goes to the swales.
