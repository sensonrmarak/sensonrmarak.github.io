# UPSC Animated: titles, hook scores and growth maths (first two videos)

> **Honest note.** Nobody can guarantee YouTube views; the algorithm decides who sees a new video. The numbers below are **estimates from a simple, transparent model**. The inputs are typical ranges for new educational channels, and every assumption is shown so you can replace it with your real YouTube Studio numbers after the first 7–28 days.

The main message of each title sits in its first ~55 characters, so it still reads well when mobile search cuts the title off (the "| UPSC…" tag is what drops off).

### Hook score (0–10)
Five checks, 0–2 points each:
- **Curiosity:** does it open a question?
- **Keyword early:** is a searched term in the first 40 characters?
- **Specific:** does it have numbers or concrete nouns?
- **Clarity:** can a student say in a second what they'll learn?
- **Length:** is the key part 60 characters or fewer?

| Title | Curiosity | Keyword early | Specific | Clarity | Length | **Score** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Latitude, Earth's Tilt, Solstices & Why Climates Differ \| UPSC Geography | 2 ("Why…") | 2 (latitude, solstices) | 1 | 2 | 1 | **8 / 10** |
| Indian Monsoon: 2 Phases, 5 Causes & How It's Changing \| UPSC | 2 ("How it's changing") | 2 (Indian Monsoon) | 2 (2, 5) | 2 | 2 | **10 / 10** |

### Hook inside the video (first 30 seconds)
YouTube shows a video to more people when viewers keep watching. **Target: at least 70% of viewers still watching at 0:30.**
Both videos open by asking their three what / why / how questions within the first 15 seconds. That opening is the hook.

## 2. Thumbnails

Files: `thumbnails/thumb_seasons_1280x720.jpg` and `thumbnails/thumb_monsoon_1280x720.jpg`. Both are 1280×720 and under 2 MB.

How they are designed to catch the eye:

| Choice | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| 2–3 big words ("WHY SEASONS?", "INDIAN MONSOON") | Still readable at phone size (about 168×94 px) |
| Dark blue or teal background with yellow and white text | Stands out against both YouTube's white and dark themes |
| One main picture (the tilted globe, or India with rain clouds) | The eye lands on one thing |
| The character's face | Faces draw attention. It also becomes your channel's mascot |
| The "23.5°" label and the WHAT? WHY? HOW? chips | Raise a question the viewer wants answered (curiosity gap) |
| Nothing important in the bottom-right corner | YouTube puts the video length there |
| Title and thumbnail use different words | The thumbnail asks the question; the title lists what you will learn |

## 3. Expected views, likes and subscribers (first 28 days, each video)

**Model:**
- **Views** = impressions × click-through rate (CTR) + views from your own shares (WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram)
- **Watch hours** = views × average view duration ÷ 60
- **Likes** = views × like rate
- **Subscribers** = views × subscribe rate

| Assumption | Low | Base | High | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Impressions (times the thumbnail is shown) | 300 | 1,500 | 8,000 | A channel with no subscribers gets small test batches. "High" means search picks the video up |
| CTR | 3% | 5% | 7% | YouTube says most channels sit between 2% and 10% |
| Views from your own shares | 20 | 100 | 400 | Sharing in UPSC Telegram and WhatsApp groups matters a lot for video #1 |
| Retention, seasons (6:40 long) | 35% (2.3 min) | 45% (3.0 min) | 55% (3.7 min) | |
| Retention, monsoon (4:48 long) | 40% (1.9 min) | 50% (2.4 min) | 60% (2.9 min) | Shorter videos keep a higher % |
| Like rate | 4% | 4% | 4% | Typical for education |
| Subscribe rate | 1% | 2% | 3% | Viewers who subscribe after watching |

**Results:**

| Per video (28 days) | Low | **Base** | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Views | 29 | **175** | 960 |
| Seasons watch hours | 1.1 | **8.8** | 59 |
| Monsoon watch hours | 0.9 | **7.0** | 46 |
| Likes | 1 | **7** | 38 |

Example (seasons, base case): 1,500 × 0.05 + 100 = **175 views** → 175 × 3.0 ÷ 60 = **8.75 hours** → 175 × 0.04 = **7 likes** → 175 × 0.02 = **3.5 subscribers**.

## 4. What changes the numbers most

| What you improve | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| CTR from 5% to 7% (better thumbnail, test titles) | **+40% views** from impressions | 75 → 105 views from 1,500 impressions |
| Retention from 45% to 55% | **+22% watch hours**, and YouTube shows the video to more people | 3.0 → 3.7 min per view |
| A playlist and end screen (watch video 2 after video 1) | +1 extra view for maybe 15–25% of viewers | +26–44 views per 175 |
| Three Shorts cut from each video (45–60 s: "Why is Jan winter if Earth is closest?", "8 states on the Tropic of Cancer", "Why Tamil Nadu gets winter rain") | Shorts reach non-subscribers; 1–3% click through to the long video | 3,000 Short views → 30–90 long views |
| Pinned comment with a past exam question (e.g. *"UPSC 2013: Which states does the Tropic of Cancer pass through?"*) | More comments, which tell YouTube people are engaged | |
| Upload 6–8 PM IST, on the same days each week | Your first viewers arrive in the first hours | |

## 5. The road to monetisation (YouTube Partner Program: 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours in 12 months)

Watch hours per view = length × retention, which is about **2.7 minutes** on average for these two.
4,000 hours = 240,000 minutes ÷ 2.7 ≈ **89,000 views**.
At a 2% subscribe rate that also gives about **1,780 subscribers**, so the watch hours are the harder target.

**Growth model:** each video keeps earning about 150 views per month (UPSC topics stay relevant), and you upload *u* videos per month.

```
total views in 12 months ≈ 150 × u × (1 + 2 + … + 12) = 150 × u × 78
needed: 150 × u × 78 ≥ 89,000  →  u ≥ 7.6 videos / month
```

| Upload rate | Videos after 12 months | Views in 12 months | Watch hours | Subscribers (2%) | Reaches the 4,000 hours? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 per week (4/month) | 48 | ~46,800 | ~2,100 | ~940 | Not yet (~month 17) |
| **2 per week (8/month)** | 96 | ~93,600 | **~4,200** | **~1,870** | **Yes, about month 12** |
| 3 per week (12/month) | 144 | ~140,000 | ~6,300 | ~2,800 | Yes, about month 9–10 |

Adding Shorts and getting one video into the top search results can shorten these timelines a lot. One hit video often brings more views than the rest of the channel combined.

## 6. Launch checklist for video #1

1. Publish the **monsoon** video first (shorter, higher hook score, with "monsoon" news every June to September). Publish the seasons video 3–4 days later, close to the 22 December solstice if you can time it, when "winter solstice" searches peak.
2. Put both videos in the playlist "UPSC Geography: Animated", with end screens linking to each other.
3. Share in 5–10 UPSC Telegram and WhatsApp groups **in the first hour**, with a question rather than just a link.
4. Post one Short a day for the next 3 days.
5. After 48 hours, check YouTube Studio → Reach (CTR) and Engagement (retention at 0:30):
   - CTR under 3%: try a different title or thumbnail. Studio's "Test & compare" can test 3 thumbnails at once.
   - Retention at 0:30 under 60%: make the opening shorter on the next video.
6. Put your real numbers into section 3 and recalculate. The model gets more accurate every week.
